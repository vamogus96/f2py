## Fool Night and the city of Poors
The world turned slowly into **tidally-locked days**: a permanent light side, where the Concord towers and clean air are, and **Fool Night**, the dark side.
Between them lies **the boundary**, a twilight belt, and the city of **Poors** is bolted to the edge of it: a stack of decks and old freighters made of people with no licence, no loan they can repay and no air they can afford.
Poors has no army. It has **the Free Fleet**: space pirates and mercenaries, sail-and-thruster galleons that raid Concord freight and sell salvage back to the city. Concord answers with the **Navy**, and hires the same Poors pilots as mercenaries when it is cheaper than losing ships.

## Torino Alta, the Improvements and cyberpsychosis
On the Light Side the Concord's capital is **Torino Alta** (Turin). Its financial spire is the **Arasaka Tower**, the old Intesa Sanpaolo skyscraper, rebuilt by the Concord as a bank, a clinic and a fortress.
The Concord sells two kinds of *Improvement*:
- **For the poor:** the **Greening Loan**. They are grown into plants (see the Verdant).
- **For the rich of Turin:** **cybernetic Improvements**: mantis blades, optic grids, reflex boosters, Sandevistan bursts, subdermal plating. Sold on the upper floors (the *Improvement Suites*), paid for by the people who built the towers.

Every implant costs a little of the person. Past a threshold the body stops obeying: **cyberpsychosis**. The tower's security staff are the clients who bought too many upgrades and had nothing left but a contract and a gun. The **Chairman** on the roof garden owns the patents on all of them, and has more plating than flesh.
Aurel can buy the same implants at the Poors ripperdoc. They help, and they make the ringing in his ears louder: his baseline cyberpsychosis lowers his sleep, and sets how far he can push in the tower.

*In-fiction names:* "Arasaka Tower" and "Intesa Sanpaolo" are placeholders chosen for this prototype (see `docs/LICENSING.md`, section Trademarks). Rename them before any public release.

## The Tower raid (Hotline-style combat)
Four floors: the Lobby, the Trading Floor, the Improvement Suites and the Chairman's Roof Garden. Rules are those of the classic top-down one-hit-kill brawler, with original art, code and names:
- **Real time**, one hit kills everybody, including you. **R** restarts the floor instantly.
- Guards have **vision cones** and **ears**: gunshots alert everyone in earshot, melee is nearly silent.
- **Doors** swing open and knock down whoever stands behind them. Bats, fists, thrown items and doors **knock enemies down**; a downed enemy can be **executed**.
- Enemies react with a delay, then are accurate. Kill chains build a **combo**, and style (executions, door stuns, thrown weapons) scores extra.
- Enemies: pistol guards, bat guards, shotgun heavies, **blade-runners** (telegraphed lunge, then stunned), **optic snipers** (laser, long aim), **psychos** (fast, erratic) and the **Chairman** (three armour plates).
- **Improvements** are the "masks": pick two, each adds cyberpsychosis. Above 40% the world glitches; above 70% there are **episodes** where the controls invert; above 100% they come often. Score is multiplied by `1 + psychosis/200`.


The Ressels were engineers. Now:
- **Low-level work:** Scrap Yard sorting (no skills; the bio crates are warm).
- **Mid-level work:** Brass Dock Workshop orders on a lathe or at the PCB bench (need skill, pay by the part, leave spare parts).
- **High-level work:** the Spire Tower contract (needs L2 in both trades, pays 50% because the licence was revoked in 2131).
- **The bus:** timetable every 20 minutes, fares by distance, strikes, inspectors who fine you for unmetered coughing.
- **Medicine and drugs:** the licensed pharmacy (Ilse's lung medicine), tinnitus drops, and the alley: counterfeit medicine (40% chalk), *Wake* (stimulant) and *Hush* (sedative, dreamless: you will not hear the echoes).
- **Cooking:** the cheapest way to feed a family. Synth-rice, algae, vat-protein, or the free **Concord greens**, which taste like someone else's garden.
- **The PCB bench and the lathe:** skills for work, and parts for **your ship**: fire-control boards and barrels. Before the Purge, Aurel's mother Ilse taught him to solder at the kitchen table. The tutorial is her lesson as the foreman repeats it: *heat both, feed the joint, solder away first, then the iron. A cold joint passes the bench test and fails in the field.*

At night the budget decides who breathes. Then Aurel dreams.

## The Captain's Watch
Aurel's recurring dream of his father's last battle. First person, on the deck of the *Lullaby*, firing the port guns at a shielded raider, then crossing a grapple bridge to a ship whose crew turn to face you when you look away.
The logbook he finds says to bring the ship home through Fool Night.

## The Void skirmish
The *Lullaby* waits at the Poors docks for a crew. Pirates and Navy mercenaries fight at the boundary: broadsides, boarding crippled ships for salvage, asteroids and the Poors station in the middle.
Pilots choose a side, **pirate** (salvage share) or **Navy mercenary** (flat Concord pay), and the money goes to the family purse. It can be played alone against bots or online with up to 8 pilots per room.


## The city, Year 47 of the Concord
The air went bad slowly and then all at once. The city survived by **metering breath**. Every citizen has an
**Oxygen Permit** with a weekly quota in litres, and every litre is taxed. The **Bureau of Breath** collects the
**Oxygen Tax** at street booths. The meters are in every stairwell, lift and bedroom.

The tax is indexed to the **Inflation Adjustment Act**, so the rate is revised during the day.
A receipt bought at 9:00 can be worthless by 14:00. Booth posters say: *OXYGEN IS A PRIVILEGE. PAY YOUR SHARE.*

## The Concord Program, the biotech breakthrough
**Concord** began as a genetics program that promised to *improve society*: crops that grow in the dark,
then the end of hunger, disease and violence. The breakthrough came from splicing plant genomes into **human volunteers**.
Volunteers became calm and cooperative, could photosynthesise, and needed less oxygen.

Then the program found its business model, the **Greening Loan**:
- 0% interest, issued to anyone who can't pay the Oxygen Tax or their debts.
- *Collateral: the biological estate of the borrower and dependants.*
- Signing makes you a **VOLUNTEER**. Most borrowers think it means a hospital.

Defaulters (and soon everyone, because inflation guarantees default) are "referred". In Concord greenhouses they are
grown into vegetables, fruit and oxygen-producing plants that feed and ventilate the wealthy districts. The poor pay for air
with loans and repay the loans with their bodies. Shareholders' gardens are enormous, and they whisper.

The half-finished results are the **Verdant**: rooted, phototropic, endlessly polite plant-people who move toward light.

## The protagonist: Aurel Ressel, Booth 9
An Oxygen Tax inspector. Years beside the Bureau's pressure pumps left him with **tinnitus**, a constant ringing at about
6 kHz. His mother lives with him, and her quota is on his bill.

### The resonance
In this world **dreams and empathy share the frequency of Aurel's tinnitus**. Every person he denies or refers leaves a
residue that rings at his pitch. Asleep, he can **tune in** (hold **R**): the world drains of colour, the ringing becomes
deafening, and the **echoes** of those people appear. Listening to them steadies his mind but costs sanity while he holds it.
The ringing was never in his ears. It is everyone he sent away, at once.

### The family
- **Ilse Ressel**, his mother: she worked the pressure pumps before him. Scarred lungs, needs medicine, always says she doesn't.
- **Nora Ressel**, his sister, 9: on the child quota. She draws plants on the wall where the window used to be.

At the end of every shift Aurel chooses who the money is for. At the inflated rate it never covers everyone.
The Greening Loan always does, and it takes the borrower *and dependants* as collateral.

## The resistance: M
A network of people who have seen the greenhouses from inside. Their courier, **Ruta Vey ("M")**, carries forged permits
because the Bureau stopped issuing seals to people like her. Letting her through earns a citation, and also a warm seed that
hums at your frequency. The dream's ending changes if you helped her.

## Lucid Raids (combat sessions)
Some nights M finds Aurel's dream at his frequency and uses it as a training ground. In the dream the resistance rehearses raids on
Concord clinics: Intake, the Greening Ward and the Nursery, where volunteers are potted. Breath is metered in the waking city,
and in the raid **time only moves when you breathe**: stand still and the world holds its breath with you.
Orderlies (red, crystalline), armed guards and half-grown Verdant shatter like glass. When you die you simply **wake up** and dream it again.

## Dream sessions
Every playthrough dreams a different **strangeness**. A seed (shown on the title screen, set with `?seed=1234`) picks a
level from 1 to 5 and that many distortions: Tilted Horizon, Child Height, Breathing Walls, Red Tide, Hue Drift,
Negative Moon, Double Vision, Long Corridors, Heavy Air, Echoing Steps, Tinnitus Storm, Breathing Light.
Use **Reroll the dream** for another.

## Story flow
1. **The Bedroom**: the lullaby, the eyes in the dark.
2. **The Hallway That Repeats**: the loop that only lets you go backwards.
3. **Waking: The Oxygen Tax Bureau**: a Papers, Please-style shift with 10 applicants, two rate hikes, Directive 7-C, the mole
   and, last in line, a man with your face holding a Greening Loan dated tomorrow. Your balance ends negative whatever you do.
4. **The Drowned House**: the memories, the Watcher.
5. **The Sleeper**: the bed at the end of the void, with someone already in it.
- *Demo:* **The Greenhouse / The Verdant**.
